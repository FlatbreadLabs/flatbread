## How to start one from inside

This is the path Tony can walk at his own employer without naming it. Steps that quote a published page say so. Steps that are ordinary workplace practice say so.

1. **Read the employment agreement before any money or public warranty.** GitHub Sponsors Additional Terms ask the sponsored developer to warrant that content does not infringe third-party intellectual property (https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-sponsors-additional-terms). Tidelift asks the same kind of contribution warranty (https://support.tidelift.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406309657876-Lifter-agreement). Many employment contracts assign inventions and restrict outside income. This file cannot read Tony’s contract and is not legal advice. If the contract is unclear, Tony asks a lawyer. Do not sign a funder warranty until that is done.

2. **Search inside the company for an OSPO, FOSS fund, peer bonus, or dependency budget.** The TODO Group says an OSPO is “a designated place where open source is supported, nurtured, shared, explained, and grown inside a company” and that it often owns strategy, contribution policy, compliance, and community spend (https://todogroup.org/resources/guides/how-to-create-an-open-source-program-office/). Search the intranet for “OSPO”, “open source program”, “FOSS fund”, “open source peer bonus”, “GitHub Sponsors”, and “Open Source Pledge”. Also search the public web for the company name plus those phrases. This search step is ordinary practice; the definition is from TODO.

3. **Check two public member lists before asking anyone to invent a programme.** Open Source Pledge members are at https://opensourcepledge.com/members/. If the company is already a member, the job is to get Proof on the next annual breakdown, not to start a pledge. If it is not a member, the join checklist is four steps: pay $2,000 per FTE developer, publish a post, open a GitHub issue, renew yearly (https://opensourcepledge.com/join/). TODO Group’s create-OSPO guide is the other public map of who usually owns this work.

4. **Find who owns the budget.** TODO’s create-OSPO guide says offices sit in legal, engineering, developer relations / marketing, or a cross-functional working group, and that the programme manager, legal, and a review board are the usual roles (https://todogroup.org/resources/guides/how-to-create-an-open-source-program-office/). Microsoft’s public OSPO page says that office makes it easy for every team to use, contribute to, and release open source (https://opensource.microsoft.com/). Bloomberg’s fund is a partnership of OSPO and Corporate Philanthropy (launch coverage). Indeed’s playbook assumes procurement must be able to pay (https://github.com/indeedeng/FOSS-Contributor-Fund). In practice, ask engineering leadership, legal / IP, and finance / procurement. That last sentence is ordinary workplace practice.

5. **Read the contribution policy before evangelising Proof at work.** TODO’s participating-in-communities guide says companies that contribute without a strategy take legal risk, and that the common successful pattern is to pay employees to participate in projects they use (https://todogroup.org/resources/guides/participating-in-open-source-communities/). Google’s public releasing guide is an example of a strict employee process: work-related releases go through review; side projects still need manager approval; pre-employment personal projects have a separate patching path (https://opensource.google/documentation/reference/releasing). Tony’s employer may have a lighter or heavier process. Do not tell colleagues to send Proof PRs on work time until that policy is found. This is not legal advice.

6. **Put receive rails in place before asking for money.** Companies will not invent a payee. Published bars:

   - Microsoft prefers GitHub Sponsors (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/foss-fund/main/README.md).
   - Indeed requires a mechanism such as Open Collective, Patreon, PayPal, GitHub Sponsors, or a foundation (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/indeedeng/FOSS-Contributor-Fund/main/README.md).
   - Open Source Pledge names thanks.dev, Open Collective, GitHub Sponsors, and ecosyste.ms Funds (https://opensourcepledge.com/about/).
   - ecosyste.ms pays where `funding.yml` points (https://funds.ecosyste.ms/overview).
   - OSC will not host a personal-account repo and prefers two admins (https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/acceptance-criteria.md).
     So: finish GitHub Sponsors if the country is supported; keep `.github/FUNDING.yml`; claim thanks.dev; move the repo under a GitHub organisation and apply to OSC if procurement will not pay an individual. Add `SECURITY.md` and a CoC before Tidelift or any foundation home (`00-proof-fit-brief.md` records both as gaps).

7. **Get Proof into a real lockfile at the employer.** Every published FOSS fund in this file that states a use rule requires the company to use the project (Microsoft, Indeed, Spotify). thanks.dev and ecosyste.ms allocate by dependency use. GitHub’s bulk-sponsor tool builds a CSV from organisation dependencies (https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/sponsoring-open-source-contributors/sponsoring-an-open-source-contributor-through-github). A slide deck is not use. An internal service that depends on `@flatbread/proof` is use. This step is the whole strategy.

8. **Ask for a payment rail, not a FOSS-fund award, if Tony is the employee-owner.** Microsoft, Indeed, Salesforce, and Spotify all publish a form of “not owned / not run / not maintained by our employee.” Indeed’s playbook says that rule exists to avoid compensation and ethics problems. If Tony’s employer copies that template, Proof will be ineligible for their FOSS fund. The asks that still work are: GitHub Sponsors from the company org, thanks.dev on the company codebase, an Open Source Pledge line, and ecosyste.ms fund sponsorship. Those rails pay dependencies. They do not vote on employee side projects.

9. **If the company has no rail, start the smallest published one.** In order of cost to the company:

   1. A single GitHub Sponsors payment from the organisation account (card; or $5,000 invoice minimum).
   2. thanks.dev connected to the company GitHub org, with a stated monthly budget.
   3. Open Source Pledge at $2,000 per FTE developer, with Proof as one named line.
   4. An Indeed-style FOSS fund only if they accept that Tony’s project may be excluded.
      Point at the live pages above. Do not invent a fourth product.

10. **Use colleague contributions on purpose.** Several programmes record a second-organisation or multi-person bar:

    - GSoC: at least 5 active people (https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/org-application) and at least two mentors (https://developers.google.com/open-source/gsoc/faq).
    - Outreachy: a small community can apply; first report recorded a three-person welcome (https://www.outreachy.org/communities/cfp/).
    - CNCF Sandbox applications are closed without a MAINTAINERS table and, on the lifecycle page, a minimum of 3 maintainers from 2+ different employers. “Organization” means employer, not a GitHub org. All maintainers from the same company count as one (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/lifecycle/). Proof is still a poor CNCF fit on licence and scope; the threshold is recorded here because it is the clearest published definition of “second contributing organisation.”
    - LF AI & Data incubation later needs 2+ organisations contributing (`internal/funding/README.md`).
    - OSC prefers two Open Collective admins.
      Ask colleagues to send real PRs, reviews, or docs under their work identity if policy allows. A star is not a contributing organisation.

11. **Use conference travel as a published employer duty, not a personal luxury.** OpenJS Community Fund policy tells applicants to explore employer-sponsored funding first. Linux Foundation Travel Fund asks people whose company can pay not to apply (https://docs.linuxfoundation.org/lfx/my-profile/lf-events/travel-funding). A short internal case for a talk is: the foundation will not pay if the employer can; the talk recruits contributors; the company already wants the tool used.

12. **Write a short internal case that copies what funded projects already said.** Keep it to one page:

    - What Proof is, in one sentence: durable, file-backed project memory for coding agents, MIT, reviewed in Git.
    - Where the company would use it (named internal repo or workflow). Without this, Microsoft/Indeed/Spotify-style rules fail.
    - What the company must do: Sponsor on GitHub, or connect thanks.dev, or join the Pledge. Include the URL and the fee or floor ($5,000 invoice; $2,000 per developer; thanks.dev budget the company chooses).
    - What Proof will spend: model-API evals across families, contractor hours, not a replacement salary.
    - What the company will not get: equity, exclusive features, a hosted product.
    - IP note: Tony will confirm he can receive the money; the company should run its own conflict check. Spotify’s MSW write-up is the closest published analogue: a solo TypeScript maintainer, funded because the library sat in the web stack (https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/1/congratulations-to-the-recipients-of-the-2025-spotify-foss-fund).
    - Do not name other employers as if they already use Proof. They do not, on repository evidence.

13. **Do not start a full OSPO in order to fund Proof.** TODO’s create guide is for companies that want a standing office (leader, operations, buy-in, policy). That is months of organisational work. It is the right long-term home for a contribution policy and a FOSS fund. It is the wrong first ask. The first ask is a payment rail and permission for colleagues to contribute.
