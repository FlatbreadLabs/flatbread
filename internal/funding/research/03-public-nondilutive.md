### EIC Pathfinder Challenges 2026 — DeepRAP: Deep Reasoning, Abstraction & Planning towards trustworthy Cognitive AI Systems

Funder — European Innovation Council, under Horizon Europe (European Commission).

Track — EIC Pathfinder Challenges 2026, challenge titled DeepRAP: Deep Reasoning, Abstraction & Planning towards trustworthy Cognitive AI Systems.

Support type — CASH, MENTORSHIP.

Award — Grants of up to EUR 4 million, or more if duly justified. The Challenges page and the Pathfinder page both state this ceiling. The official Pathfinder page also lists booster grants of a fixed amount not exceeding EUR 50 000 for testing commercialisation potential, plus coaching, mentoring, and networking. The Challenges budget for 2026 is €96 million. The money pays for early-stage scientific and technological R&D (the Pathfinder page states TRL 1–4, up to proof of concept). The 20 May 2026 Pathfinder Open news item restates funded Pathfinder projects as grants of up to €3–4 million.

Strings attached — Standard Horizon Europe grant agreement. The Pathfinder page lists Business Acceleration Services after award. No equity is stated on the Pathfinder page. Reporting and consortium rules follow Horizon Europe. A 22-page Part B is required for Pathfinder Open in the 2026 work programme PDF; Challenges follow the same work programme. Travel or residency is not stated as a condition on the pages opened.

Applicant type required — A legal entity established in an EU Member State or a Horizon Europe Associated Country. Pathfinder Challenges accept a single applicant, a two-partner consortium, or a larger consortium. The official Pathfinder FAQ states single legal entities established in a Member State or Associated Country may apply to Challenges, but mid-caps and larger companies are not eligible as single applicants. Consortia of at least three independent legal entities in different countries remain the default Pathfinder Open rule; Challenges relax that. Natural persons can appear in some Pathfinder settings (work programme text). An unincorporated project with no legal entity cannot sign a Horizon grant. Forming an eligible entity means incorporating a company, non-profit, or research organisation in a Member State or Associated Country (the UK is associated to the whole of Horizon Europe including Pathfinder, per the official FAQ; Swiss entities have been eligible for Pathfinder since 10 November 2025, per the same FAQ). That incorporation, a PIC on the Funding & Tenders Portal, and a bank account in the entity’s name are the minimum. An individual acting alone, with no entity, cannot apply.

Status — OPEN as of 23 August 2026 for Challenges. Pathfinder Open 2026 is closed (deadline 12 May 2026).

Deadline — 28 October 2026 for Pathfinder Challenges.

How to apply —

1. Confirm an eligible legal entity in a Member State or Associated Country, or form one and obtain a PIC.
2. Read the DeepRAP Challenge Guide PDF linked from the Challenges 2026 page (the guide itself was not successfully opened in this run; the page lists it).
3. Write the proposal against the Challenge’s science-towards-technology test: a long-term vision of a radically new technology, a concrete breakthrough, and a high-risk / high-gain method.
4. Submit on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal before 28 October 2026.
5. Evaluation results are expected on the Pathfinder Open cadence of about five months from the deadline (that timing is stated for Open; Challenges timing is not separately stated on the pages opened).

Cash scores 40 because the published ceiling is above $500k. Credits score 0; no model or cloud credits are stated. Exposure scores 10 because DeepRAP is aimed at a European cognitive-AI research community, which overlaps agent-memory work. Research scores 10 because Pathfinder pays for early research and evaluation, not only shipping features.

Confidence — 8 (VERY LOW). The 2025 Pathfinder Challenges call received 667 proposals and awarded 30 projects (€118 million). That is about 4.5 percent. The 2026 Challenges budget is €96 million, so a similar bar is the honest expectation. The project has no known EU or associated-country entity, no academic affiliation, and no consortium. No DeepRAP-specific acceptance rate was published on the pages opened.

DeepRAP is the only 2026 Pathfinder Challenge whose stated aim (deep reasoning, abstraction, planning, trustworthy cognitive AI) matches Proof’s agent-memory research agenda, but only a legal entity in a Member State or Associated Country can apply, and the bar is a science-towards-technology breakthrough, not a shipping open-source tool.

Pitch angle — Proof keeps an agent’s reasons beside the code as typed Markdown records under `.flatbread-proof/`. An Effort holds one thread of work. Issue, Finding, Decision, Constraint, Risk, Citation, and Blob records sit under it, linked by `derives_from`, `supersedes`, `invalidates`, and `cites`. The grant would pay for a controlled study of whether those typed edges change a later agent’s decisions, and whether the digest caps still hold as the graph grows. ### NSF CISE Future Computing Research (Future CoRe) — Robust Intelligence or Software and Hardware Foundations

Funder — United States National Science Foundation, Directorate for Computer and Information Science and Engineering.

Track — NSF 25-543, Computer and Information Science and Engineering: Future Computing Research (Future CoRe). The closest named programmes inside that solicitation are Robust Intelligence (RI) and Software and Hardware Foundations (SHF). Human-Centered Computing (HCC) and Information Integration and Informatics (III) are also in the same solicitation.

Support type — CASH.

Award — Up to $1,000,000 for up to 4 years. The solicitation says typical projects are about $150,000 to $250,000 per year for 3 to 4 years, and projects are discouraged from exceeding $300,000 in any single year. The money pays for research and education that develops new knowledge in computing. No cost sharing is allowed.

Strings attached — Standard NSF reporting (annual project reports and a public project outcomes report). No equity. Intellectual property follows NSF grant terms. No residency is stated. A PI may be on a limited number of Future CoRe proposals (the solicitation enforces a per-person proposal limit; the exact count is in the solicitation text).

Applicant type required — Only (1) US-accredited institutions of higher education with a US campus, acting on behalf of faculty; (2) US non-profit, non-academic organisations associated with education or research; (3) federally recognized Tribal Nations. For-profit companies cannot submit. Individuals and unincorporated projects cannot submit. Forming a US company does not open this door. The only path is a faculty appointment, a staff role that can PI through a US university or qualifying non-profit, or a university that agrees to lead with the maintainer as senior personnel. An overseas campus of a US university is not enough without a US-campus justification.

Status — OPEN. Proposals are accepted at any time. Target dates steer panel assignment.

Deadline — Next target date after 23 August 2026 is the second Thursday in September annually (the 2025 date printed in the solicitation is 11 September 2025; the next such Thursday in 2026 is 10 September 2026). The following target is the first Thursday in February annually (5 February 2026 is printed; the next is 4 February 2027). Submit by a target date if a panel in that cycle is wanted.

How to apply —

1. Secure a US university or qualifying non-profit as the submitting organisation, and a PI who can submit through it.
2. Contact a cognizant CISE programme officer for RI or SHF before writing.
3. Prepare a full NSF proposal in Research.gov or Grants.gov against NSF 25-543 and the PAPPG in force on the submission date.
4. Submit at any time; aim at 10 September 2026 for the next panel cycle.

Opportunity value — 58. cash/credits/exposure/research = 40/0/8/10.
Cash scores 40 at the $1M ceiling. Credits score 0. Exposure scores 8 because RI and SHF panels sit inside the US academic CS and AI community, which includes agent and software researchers, not a product-adoption audience. Research scores 10 because this is a research programme.

Confidence — 6 (VERY LOW). NSF did not publish an acceptance rate on the pages opened. The project has no known US academic affiliation. Without that affiliation the score is 0. With a willing US university PI, a solo 64-star tool still has to be recast as new knowledge in intelligence or software foundations, not as product maintenance. That is a steep recast. Paperwork for a full NSF research proposal is large; it is only worth the time if the university already wants the work.

Pitch angle — Proof is a typed relational graph for an agent’s durable reasons, stored as ordinary files in the user’s Git repository. The research question is what structure an agent’s memory should have, how to keep recall bounded as the graph grows, and whether stored reasoning changes a later agent’s behaviour. That is a Robust Intelligence and Software Foundations question, not a product pitch. The grant would pay for instrumented recall experiments across three model families, graph-size stress tests of the 25-record / 64 KiB digest, and a public dataset of agent traces. The deliverable is papers and open artefacts a CISE programme can point to as new knowledge about agent memory, not a hosted service.

### EIC Accelerator Open — grant-only route

Funder — European Innovation Council, under Horizon Europe.

Track — EIC Accelerator Open 2026 (any field of technology). Challenges exist as a parallel track with a 2026 budget of €220 million; Open has €414 million.

Support type — CASH. EQUITY-INVESTMENT is optional (blended finance or investment-only). Grant-only is available once during Horizon Europe 2021–2027.

Award — Lump-sum grant component below €2.5 million for innovation activities at TRL 6–8, to be finished within 24 months. Investment component €1–€10 million if chosen. UK applicants may apply only for grant-only.

Strings attached — Grant-only avoids equity. Blended finance and investment-only take equity or quasi-equity through the EIC Fund and add due diligence of 2–6 months. Three unsuccessful submissions at any stage bar further Accelerator applications for the rest of Horizon Europe. Coaching and mentoring are offered. A 3-minute video, a 10-slide deck, and later a 20-page full proposal are required. Interview with an EIC jury if shortlisted.

Applicant type required — A single for-profit start-up or SME established in an EU Member State or Horizon Europe Associated Country. Natural persons or legal entities willing to set up an SME may start, but a qualifying company must exist before the full application (Step 2) if relocating from a third country, and before grant signature in any case. Small mid-caps (up to 499 employees) may apply for investment-only, not the grant. Consortia are not allowed. An unincorporated project cannot apply. Forming an eligible SME means incorporating a for-profit company that meets the EU SME definition (under 250 staff and either ≤ €50 million turnover or ≤ €43 million balance sheet — the last two figures appear on second-hand guides, not on the official Accelerator page opened here; treat the exact SME thresholds as UNVERIFIED against the official page). The official page requires a start-up or SME (including spin-outs) in a Member State or Associated Country. Third-country applicants may relocate the company before Step 2.

Status — OPEN. Short proposals are accepted at any time and batched on the first Tuesday of each month.

Deadline — Next full-proposal batching dates still ahead: 2 September 2026 and 4 November 2026, 17:00 Brussels time. Short proposals have no closing date.

How to apply —

1. Incorporate (or relocate) a for-profit SME in a Member State or Associated Country.
2. Submit a short proposal at any time on the Funding & Tenders Portal: 12-page form, 10-slide deck, 3-minute video of up to three team members.
3. If the short proposal receives a GO (about 4–6 weeks), prepare the 20-page full proposal, implementation plan, financials, letters of intent, FTO analysis, and video, and hit a 2026 batching date.
4. If invited, attend the jury interview after every second batch.
5. Grant-only: sign the grant agreement and take first pre-financing.

Opportunity value — 52. cash/credits/exposure/research = 40/0/8/4.
Cash scores 40 at the grant ceiling. Credits score 0. Exposure scores 8 because Accelerator winners are shown as European deep-tech scale-ups, which reaches some AI founders, not TypeScript library users. Research scores 4 because the grant is for TRL 6–8 innovation and scale-up, not basic memory-graph research.

Confidence — 5 (VERY LOW). The official page does not publish an acceptance rate. A 15 June 2026 news item says 38 start-ups and SMEs were funded in the latest round; a 17 February 2026 item says 61 were funded in an earlier 2026 round. Applicant counts for those rounds were not on the pages opened. The project has no company, no paid tier, no hosted product, and no scale-up plan. Accelerator asks for a market-disrupting product. Proof is a file-based open-source tool. Even after forming an EU SME, the commercial story is a mismatch. Paperwork (video, FTO, jury) is heavier than a first grant should cost a solo maintainer unless a real company already exists.

Fit verdict — WEAK. Grant-only cash is large and non-dilutive, but the programme funds SMEs scaling a TRL 6–8 product, and Proof is not that company today.

Pitch angle — Do not pitch this unless a for-profit SME exists and the team is ready to sell a product. If that happens, the pitch is: coding agents forget why they chose what they chose; Proof stores that reasoning in the user’s Git repository as typed records with bounded recall. The Accelerator grant would pay to harden the write path, ship evaluation harnesses as a product feature, and take the tool from a maintainer release to a supported offering European developer-tool buyers can adopt. The deliverable is a market-ready release with paying or design-partner users, not a paper.

### NSF Pathways to Enable Secure Open-Source Ecosystems (PESOSE)

Funder — United States National Science Foundation, Directorate for Technology, Innovation and Partnerships, with other NSF directorates listed on the solicitation.

Track — NSF 26-506, Pathways to Enable Secure Open-Source Ecosystems. Three tracks: Track 1 scoping and planning; Track 2 establishing and expanding an open-source ecosystem; Track 3 improving safety, security, and privacy of an existing OSE. This replaces NSF 24-606 POSE, which no longer accepts applications.

Support type — CASH, MENTORSHIP.

Award — Track 1: maximum $300,000 for up to 1 year, about 30 awards. Track 2: maximum $1,500,000 for up to 2 years, about 10 awards. Track 3: maximum $1,500,000 for up to 2 years, about 10 awards. Anticipated programme funding $40,000,000; 40 to 60 awards. Track 1 pays for planning and training in governance, licensing, fundraising, and administration, not product development. Track 2 pays to build governance, community, security, and long-term viability around an existing open-source product. Track 3 pays to find and fix vulnerabilities. No cost sharing.

Strings attached — Track 1 and Track 2 awardees must join mandatory “I-Corps for PESOSE” experiential activities. Standard NSF annual reports plus a public outcomes report. CHIPS and Science Act person-or-entity-of-concern rules apply. For-profit recipients use FAR Part 31 cost principles. Three to five letters of collaboration from current users or contributors who are not on the proposing team are required. SAM.gov UEI is required and can take weeks.

Applicant type required — A US organisation from this list: US-based for-profit commercial organisations including small businesses with research or education capability; US non-profit non-academic organisations tied to education or research; other federal agencies and FFRDCs (with PAPPG limits); state and local governments; US-accredited institutions of higher education with a US campus; federally recognized Tribal Nations. Individuals and unincorporated projects cannot submit. Non-profit and for-profit proposers must be US-based and US-owned and controlled: more than 50 percent of equity owned by US citizens, permanent residents, or firms that themselves meet that test, and actual control must sit in that block. The PI at a non-university organisation must be an employee normally resident in the US. University PIs must hold a tenure-track post, a primary full-time research or teaching appointment, or a staff leadership role in an Open-Source Program Office at a US campus. Forming a qualifying US company means incorporating in a US state, putting majority ownership and control in US citizens or permanent residents, employing a US-resident PI, registering a SAM.gov UEI, and opening a US bank account in the company’s name. That is weeks to months of work before a proposal can be filed.

Status — OPEN.

Deadline — 1 September 2026, 17:00 local time of the submitting organisation. Then the first Tuesday in September annually, and 2 March 2027 then the first Tuesday in March annually.

How to apply —

1. Decide Track 1 (planning) unless three to five independent user or contributor letters and a managing-organisation plan already exist for Track 2.
2. Form or partner with an eligible US organisation and finish SAM.gov registration.
3. Collect three to five letters of collaboration from current users or contributors not on the team.
4. Write the proposal (7 pages for Track 1, 15 for Track 2 or 3). Title must start `PESOSE: Track N:`.
5. Submit through Research.gov or Grants.gov under NSF 26-506.
6. Budget I-Corps for PESOSE time for Track 1 or 2.

Opportunity value — 46. cash/credits/exposure/research = 34/0/8/4.
Scored on Track 1, the honest first award ($300,000 sits in the $100k–$500k cash band). Track 2 or 3 would raise cash to 40. Credits score 0. Exposure scores 8 because PESOSE is built to grow an open-source user and contributor community, including AI and cloud tooling, not a general public audience. Research scores 4 because Track 1 is planning, not evaluation science; later tracks fund ecosystem work more than recall experiments.

Confidence — 12 (VERY LOW). NSF did not publish an applicant count or acceptance rate on the pages opened. About 30 Track 1 awards are expected from a national open-source call. The project is a 64-star solo-maintained tool with no named managing organisation, no formal governance file, and no known US entity. Track 2’s letters-from-current-users test is hard at that scale. Track 1 is the only honest first shot, and only after a US-owned organisation exists. A full NSF proposal plus I-Corps is a large time cost; it is worth it at $300,000, not as a speculative weekend application.

PESOSE wants managing organisations that turn research-grade open-source products into safe, governed ecosystems. Track 1 money would pay the maintainer to write governance, contributor, security-disclosure, and sustainability plans, run the I-Corps for PESOSE discovery work, and recruit the external users who would later support a Track 2 proposal. The deliverable is a published OSE plan and a small, named contributor and user group, not a hosted service.
